# Publications Taxonomy

How we describe and file every document Talitha Kum RAHT Kenya publishes on
talithakumraht.org.

This guide exists so that two different people classifying the same document end
up with the same answer. If you are unsure about anything here, ask the
Coordinator (`<coordinator email>`) before publishing — do not guess.

---

## 1. The one-line rule

Three different labels do three different jobs. Keep them separate.

| Label | Answers | How many |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type** | *What kind of document is this?* | Exactly **one** |
| **Theme** | *What is it about?* | **One to three** |
| **Keywords** | *What specific things are named in it?* | Free-form, as many as are genuinely useful (aim 3-8) |

Said in one line: **Type is the shape of the document, Theme is the subject of
the document, Keywords are the specifics inside the document.**

Worked example — a newsletter issue about a school outreach in Kisumu:

- Type: `Newsletter`
- Theme: `Prevention`, `Youth & Schools`
- Keywords: `Kisumu`, `secondary schools`, `boda boda riders`, `parish youth groups`

Note that "Kisumu" is a keyword, not a theme. Places, partner organisations and
named groups are always keywords.

---

## 2. Type — the default list

Pick **exactly one**. If a document honestly fits two Types, pick the one a
reader would name first if you showed them the cover, and put the second one in
Keywords.

| Type | Use it when the document is... |
| --- | --- |
| **Annual Report** | The organisation's own account of a full year of work — activities, reach, finances and direction. |
| **Research & Data** | A study, survey, baseline, mapping or evaluation that presents findings gathered through a method (interviews, field data, desk review). |
| **Policy Brief** | A short document arguing for a specific change in law, policy or practice, aimed at decision-makers. |
| **Newsletter** | A recurring dated round-up of news, activities and updates from the network. |
| **Training Manual** | Instructional material designed to be taught from or worked through — curricula, facilitator guides, workshop handbooks. |
| **Awareness Material** | Public-facing material designed to inform or warn a general audience — posters, flyers, fact sheets, radio scripts, campaign one-pagers. |
| **Prayer & Reflection** | Liturgical or devotional material — prayer guides, novenas, reflections, homily notes, World Day of Prayer resources. |
| **Press Release** | A statement issued to media on a specific date about a specific event, position or announcement. |
| **Conference Paper** | A paper, presentation or formal address prepared for a conference, assembly, symposium or synod. |

**Common judgement calls**

- A fact sheet with statistics in it is `Awareness Material`, not `Research & Data`,
  unless we ourselves collected the data.
- A workshop report (what happened at a training) is `Newsletter` if it is part of
  a regular round-up, otherwise `Research & Data` if it evaluates outcomes. The
  training *materials* used at that workshop are `Training Manual`.
- A statement of position that is not sent to media is `Policy Brief`, not
  `Press Release`.

## 3. Theme — the default list

Pick **one to three**. Almost every publication gets at least one of the four Ps.

### 3.1 The four Ps (the spine)

These come from the Talitha Kum network's own framework and are the top level of
our theme list. If a publication has only one theme, it should normally be one of
these four.

| Theme | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Prevention** | Stopping trafficking before it happens — awareness raising, education, community sensitisation, safe recruitment information. |
| **Protection** | Direct response to people who have been trafficked or are at risk — rescue, shelter, care, reintegration, legal support. |
| **Partnership** | Working with others — government, police, church structures, other NGOs, international networks, cross-border coordination. |
| **Prayer** | The spiritual life of the network — prayer, liturgy, reflection, formation of religious and lay members. |

### 3.2 Topical themes

Use these alongside a P to say what the publication is specifically about.

| Theme | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Child Trafficking** | Trafficking of people under 18 — child labour, child marriage, trafficking of minors, orphan and street-connected children. |
| **Labour Exploitation** | Forced labour and exploitative work, including domestic work, agriculture, fishing, construction and overseas labour migration. |
| **Safe Migration** | Safe movement across borders and within Kenya — migrant workers, recruitment agencies, returnee support, Gulf corridor issues. |
| **Survivor Care** | The practical and psychological care of survivors — trauma-informed support, counselling, livelihoods, family reunification. |
| **Digital Safety** | Online recruitment and grooming, social media risks, online scams and job adverts, safe phone and internet use. |
| **Youth & Schools** | Work with young people and educational institutions — school clubs, peer educators, teacher training, university outreach. |
| **Advocacy** | Influencing law, policy and public institutions; campaigns, submissions, coalition statements. |
| **Faith Formation** | Teaching and formation grounded in faith — catechesis, Catholic social teaching, formation of sisters and seminarians. |

**Rule of thumb:** if you find yourself wanting a fourth theme, one of the three
you already have is probably a keyword instead.

## 4. Fields for every publication

| Field | Required? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title** | Required | See section 5. |
| **Summary / abstract** | Required | Roughly **40-60 words**. See section 6. |
| **Cover image** | Required | See section 8. If there is no designed cover, follow the fallback rule there. |
| **PDF file** | Required | See section 7 for naming. |
| **Publication date** | Required | The date on the document itself, not the date you uploaded it. If the document shows only a month, use the first day of that month. |
| **Issuing body** | Required | Who published it. Default: `Talitha Kum RAHT Kenya`. Name co-publishers in full, e.g. `Talitha Kum RAHT Kenya and <partner name>`. |
| **Language** | Required | One of: English, Kiswahili, French. If the same document exists in two languages, upload it twice as two separate publications and cross-reference them in the summary. |
| **Page count** | Optional | Helpful for readers on limited data. Count the PDF pages including the cover. |
| **File size** | Optional | Usually filled in automatically at upload. If entering by hand, use MB to one decimal place, e.g. `2.4 MB`. |
| **Featured flag** | Optional | Off by default. Keep no more than **three** featured publications at a time, otherwise nothing stands out. |
| **Keywords** | Recommended | Free-form and specific: counties and towns, partner and congregation names, target groups, campaign names, event names. Lower case except proper nouns. Reuse existing keywords rather than inventing near-duplicates (`boda boda`, not `bodaboda` and `boda-boda`). |

## 5. Title conventions

A good title tells a reader what the document is and, where relevant, where and
when it applies. Rules:

- Say the subject plainly. Do not rely on the cover image to explain the title.
- Include the year or period for anything dated (reports, newsletters, studies).
- Include the place if the document is about a specific county or region.
- Do not put the Type in the title unless it is genuinely part of the name
  (`Annual Report` and `Newsletter` usually are; `Policy Brief` usually is not).
- No ALL CAPS. No trailing full stop. Spell out abbreviations the first time.
- Keep it under about 12 words.

**Good**

- `Annual Report 2025: Walking with Survivors`
- `Safe Labour Migration to the Gulf: A Guide for Families in Machakos County`
- `Sauti Yetu Newsletter, March 2026`

**Bad**

- `Report` — which report, about what, from when?
- `TK RAHT KE AR FY25 FINAL v3` — internal filename, not a public title.
- `A Study` — tells the reader nothing and sorts uselessly in a list.

## 6. Writing the summary

The summary is what a reader sees before deciding whether to download. On a
phone with limited data, that decision matters. Write **40-60 words** — about
three or four sentences.

Your summary must answer four questions:

1. **What is this document?** ("This newsletter...", "This training manual...")
2. **Who is it for?** (families, school teachers, parish leaders, policymakers)
3. **What will the reader learn or find inside?** (the two or three most useful things)
4. **Where and when does it apply?** (county, region, year — if relevant)

Practical guidance:

- Write it as if explaining to a friend who asked "what's this one about?"
- Use ordinary words. If you must use a term like *reintegration*, add three
  words of explanation: "reintegration — helping survivors return home".
- Do not copy the first paragraph of the document. It is usually a greeting or a
  background statement and tells the reader nothing.
- Do not write "This important document..." or "This must-read report..." Say
  what is in it and let the reader decide.
- Never put a survivor's name, story detail or identifying description in a
  summary. See section 9.

**Example (54 words)**

> A practical guide for families in Machakos County whose relatives are taking
> up work in Gulf countries. It explains how to check that a recruitment agency
> is licensed, which documents to keep copies of before travel, warning signs of
> a fake job offer, and who to call in Kenya if contact is lost.

## 7. Naming the PDF files

Use this pattern, all lower case, hyphens only, no spaces, no underscores, no
apostrophes or commas:

```
raht-<type>-<short-title>-<yyyy-mm>.pdf
```

- `<type>` — a short slug of the Type: `annual-report`, `research`, `policy-brief`,
  `newsletter`, `training-manual`, `awareness`, `prayer`, `press-release`,
  `conference-paper`.
- `<short-title>` — two to four words from the title, lower case, hyphenated.
- `<yyyy-mm>` — the publication year and month, e.g. `2026-03`.

**Examples**

| Publication | Filename |
| --- | --- |
| Annual Report 2025: Walking with Survivors | `raht-annual-report-walking-with-survivors-2026-02.pdf` |
| Sauti Yetu Newsletter, March 2026 | `raht-newsletter-sauti-yetu-2026-03.pdf` |
| Safe Labour Migration to the Gulf (Machakos) | `raht-awareness-safe-labour-migration-machakos-2026-01.pdf` |
| Kiswahili version of the same guide | `raht-awareness-safe-labour-migration-machakos-sw-2026-01.pdf` |

Notes:

- For non-English files, add a language suffix before the date: `-sw` for
  Kiswahili, `-fr` for French. English needs no suffix.
- For a corrected re-issue, add `-v2` at the end:
  `raht-newsletter-sauti-yetu-2026-03-v2.pdf`. Never reuse the v1 filename.
- Do not include the words `final`, `draft`, `latest` or a person's initials.

## 8. Cover images

| Setting | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| **Preferred size** | **1200 x 1600 pixels, portrait** (3:4). This matches the shape of a document and looks right in the publications grid. |
| **Alternative** | **1200 x 630 pixels, landscape** if you are reusing a social media card. Do not mix shapes within the same set of publications if you can avoid it. |
| **Format** | JPEG for photographic covers, PNG for covers that are mostly text or flat colour. WebP is fine if your tool produces it. |
| **File size ceiling** | **300 KB maximum.** Compress before uploading. Large covers make the page slow on mobile data. |
| **Alt text** | Required. See section 10. |

**If there is no designed cover**

Do not upload a blank grey box, and do not screenshot a page of the PDF at low
quality. In order of preference:

1. Export page 1 of the PDF as an image at 1200 x 1600 and use that, provided
   the page is a real cover with the title on it.
2. Use a clean, well-lit photograph from the related activity, cropped to
   portrait — **only if it clears the safeguarding rules in section 9.**
3. Use the organisation's standard cover template: logo, the publication title,
   the year, and the Type, on a plain background.

Never use a stock photo of a distressed woman or child as a cover. It is
inaccurate, it stereotypes survivors, and it invites people to read the document
as a story about victims rather than about our work.

## 9. Safeguarding and consent

We publish about human trafficking. A careless detail in a PDF can put a real
person at risk. These rules are not optional, and they apply to the cover image,
the summary, the keywords and the document itself.

**Never publish without documented written consent:**

- Photographs in which a survivor is recognisable — face, distinctive clothing,
  tattoo, home, or workplace.
- Any photograph of a person under 18 in which they are recognisable, including
  school and group photos. Consent for a minor comes from a parent or legal
  guardian, in writing, and the young person must also agree.
- Names, initials, ages combined with locations, family details, court case
  numbers, or any combination that would let someone in the person's community
  identify them.

"Documented written consent" means a signed form, held on file, that states what
the material will be used for and that it will appear publicly online. Verbal
agreement at the time of the photo is not enough. If you cannot produce the form,
you do not have consent.

**Always do these before publishing:**

- **Anonymise case studies.** Change the name and say so ("*Names have been
  changed*"). Remove the exact village, the employer's name, the specific date of
  rescue, and the number of siblings. Keep the county-level location at most.
- **Blur or crop faces** where consent is absent but the photo is still useful.
  Photographs taken from behind, or of hands, materials and settings, are safer
  and often better.
- **Strip document metadata.** A Word or PDF file carries the author name, the
  organisation, the original filename and sometimes edit history. Before upload,
  open the PDF, go to File > Properties, clear the Author and Company fields, and
  set a proper Title (see section 10). If your tool has "Sanitise document" or
  "Remove hidden information", run it.
- **Remove precise location data for shelters and safe houses.** No street
  addresses, no plot numbers, no landmarks that identify the building, no
  photographs of the exterior or gate, and no GPS coordinates. Check that photos
  taken on a phone have had their location data removed — resaving the image
  through a photo editor usually clears it. Refer to a shelter by county or by a
  general phrase such as "a partner shelter in western Kenya".
- **Check the annexes.** Consent forms, attendance lists, phone numbers and
  signatures often survive into an appendix that nobody re-read.

**When in doubt, route it up.** If you are unsure whether something identifies a
person, whether a consent form covers online use, or whether a photo is safe to
publish — stop and send it to the Coordinator (`<coordinator email>`) before
publishing. Waiting a day costs nothing. A published PDF is copied, cached and
shared within minutes and cannot be fully recalled.

## 10. Accessibility

People read our publications on cheap phones, on slow connections, and sometimes
with a screen reader. Three things make the biggest difference.

**1. PDFs must have selectable text, not scanned images.**
If you cannot select and copy a sentence in the PDF, it is a picture of a page.
Screen readers cannot read it, search engines cannot index it, and readers cannot
copy a phone number out of it. Export the PDF from the original Word, Publisher
or Canva file rather than scanning a printout. If you only have a scan, run OCR
(optical character recognition — software that converts a picture of text into
real text) before uploading.

**2. Set a meaningful document title in the PDF properties.**
In the PDF, File > Properties > Title. Put the real publication title there, e.g.
`Sauti Yetu Newsletter, March 2026`. Browsers show this in the tab instead of the
filename, and screen readers announce it. While you are there, clear the Author
field as described in section 9.

**3. Every cover image needs alt text.**
Alt text is the short description that a screen reader reads aloud in place of
the image. Describe what the image *is*, in about 10-15 words.

- Good: `Cover of the 2025 Annual Report, showing sisters walking along a rural path.`
- Bad: `image1.jpg`, `cover`, or leaving the field blank.

Other habits worth keeping: use real headings in the source document rather than
just making text bigger, keep dark text on a light background, and write link
text that says where it goes ("read the 2025 Annual Report") rather than
"click here".
